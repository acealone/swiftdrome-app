# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

## [0.26.0] - 2026-08-23

- Up Next now holds the rest of the record you're playing as well as the songs you queued by hand -- one list, with every row draggable and removable by swiping. Dragging one of the album's own songs above the ones you queued adopts it: it keeps the place you put it in, and it stays with you when you start playing something else, exactly like a song you queued yourself. What the app gathers on its own still has its own section below.
- Rearranging the queue now starts fetching straight away. Drag a song that isn't on the device yet to the front and its download begins there and then, instead of waiting for the current song to finish.
- With shuffle on, the rest of the record and the automatically gathered songs are shuffled separately, so the album you put on finishes before the endless queue takes over -- rather than an automatic pick landing between two of its songs.
- Removing one of the album's own songs from the queue no longer counts as turning down an automatic pick, so the endless queue is free to offer it again later. Only songs removed from the gathered section are remembered as unwanted, which was always the intent.
- Podcast episodes no longer show a download bar behind the playhead. Episodes are streamed and let go of -- nothing is kept on the device and nothing is fetched ahead of them -- so the layer that fills as a song arrives was describing a download that never becomes anything, and disappeared with the episode when it ended. Songs are unchanged.

## [0.25.0] - 2026-08-23

- Every song's long-press menu -- on albums, playlists, artists, search, favorites, downloads, the songs list and the queue -- gains "Start Radio": the song plays right away and the queue fills itself with similar music from your server, the way the endless queue already follows what you put on. Songs you had queued by hand survive and play after it. Podcast episodes don't offer it -- nothing is ever queued up behind an episode.
- The endless queue runs out of road less often. It has always asked the server for songs similar to what's playing and fallen back to other songs by the same artists; now, when the artists have nothing left either, it draws random songs from the same genre, and when there's no genre to draw on, random songs from the whole library -- so a radio started from an obscure song keeps playing instead of going quiet. Podcast episodes are never among what it picks up.

## [0.24.0] - 2026-08-23

- The player's quote-bubble button now does what it always looked like it should: it opens the song's lyrics, arranged the way the queue is -- the cover shrinks up beside the title and the words take the screen. Lyrics the server has with timings (a `.lrc` file beside the song, or timed tags) follow the music: the line being sung is lit, the rest stand dimmed, the view scrolls along as the song advances -- and tapping any line takes playback exactly there. Lyrics without timings are simply there to read, and a song with none says so. Skipping to another song while the words are up swaps them for the new song's.
- A seek that needs a moment now looks like one that is landing rather than one that was ignored. The playhead stays where you asked instead of dropping back to where the music still is, and once the wait is long enough to be worth a word, "Seeking…" appears in the row above the bar -- the same slot "Buffering…" uses. The highlight that sweeps along the bar while a song arrives also survives a seek instead of quietly stopping until the next song.
- The seek bar shows how much of the song is on the device. Behind the playhead, a dim layer fills as the song arrives -- for a song being fetched before it starts, and now also for one being streamed while it plays -- with a highlight sweeping along it for as long as bytes are still coming in. Once the whole song is on the device the layer stays, full width and still, so the bar goes on saying the song is yours.
- The seek bar can be dragged whenever a song is loaded, and thickens under your finger while you drag it so the spot you are aiming at isn't hidden by the fingertip aiming at it. It used to refuse to move at all while a song was still being fetched. Asking for a position during that wait now remembers where you asked for and starts the song there when the download lands, and seeking a song that is already sounding from a growing download works like any other seek. A seek that lands inside the part already fetched no longer throws that download away either: it carries on in the background, finishes the song into your downloads, and the bar goes on filling while it does.
- Putting on an album that isn't on the device no longer leaves a gap where the first song runs into the second. The song you are listening to is collected as it streams, and while that is arriving the app deliberately doesn't fetch anything else -- two transfers from a server that transcodes as it sends are two jobs competing, and the song being listened to has the better claim. That wait was supposed to end 90 seconds before the track does, so the next one is on the device in time to be joined on to. Nothing ever checked the clock, though: the check only ran when a transfer settled or a track changed, so on a link that took most of a song to hand it over, the wait ran to the end of the track, the next song was never fetched ahead, and the album boundary got the full load-it-after-the-music-stops pause instead of a gapless join. The deadline is now read off the playback clock, so it does what it says. The same wait is also lifted properly when a collection ends with nothing kept -- seeking mid-song abandons it, and that ending used to leave the queue of downloads stalled until the next track change, which was the boundary it was fetching for.
- Long-press the server row on the Server screen for a menu with "Check Connection", which pings the server there and then: the dot gives way to a spinner while it runs and comes back with the fresh answer. For when the dot is red and you have just brought the server back up.
- The library knows what a podcast is. Any album whose files carry a genre naming "podcast" -- "Podcast", "Podcasts", "Tech Podcast" -- is treated as a show: the Library tab gets a Podcasts section listing them, and a show the server split into several albums because episodes name guests as artists appears once, under one cover, with all of its episodes. Opening a show lists episodes newest first, and playing one plays it and then the episodes released after it, in order, to the newest -- and stops there. Nothing is queued up behind a podcast: the endless queue's gathered songs, crossfade and the gapless join all stand down while an episode plays, and come back for music.
- Playing an episode gets podcast controls: the player swaps shuffle and repeat out for skip-to-previous/next episode on the outside and a 15-second step back and forward either side of the play button. Episodes always stream -- even on a connection judged too slow for streaming music, where songs are downloaded before they play, since on an hour-long episode that meant a long dead wait -- and they are not kept on the device afterwards or fetched ahead, so a show doesn't eat the cache. Seeking far ahead in a streamed episode now lands instead of waiting for the rest of the download, on both engines.
- Hearting a show works like hearting a record, wherever the show was opened from. The heart lights if any of the show's server-side pieces is starred, starring or unstarring applies to all of them, and it reads back correctly on the next visit instead of waiting for a sync. Favorites keep music and podcasts apart: Home gets a Favorite Podcasts shelf beside the Favorites shelf, the Library gets a Favorite Podcasts row, and hearted shows no longer appear among favorite albums -- or several times over when episodes had guests. Unhearting removes the show from the shelves the moment you're back on them.
- Podcasts stay out of the music shelves: Most Played and Random Albums don't offer shows, and playing episodes doesn't put a show's host -- or a guest -- into Top Artists.
- Playing a song replaces the automatically gathered queue instead of topping it up: the similar-songs store is thrown away and refilled from what you just started, so the queue follows the new song rather than the old one. This also fixes the queue sometimes not gathering at all after a quick change of songs, most visibly on the render engine.
- Track numbers up to three digits fit their column in the album track list instead of wrapping onto a second line.
- The Now Playing source line says "Stream" while the render engine plays a song that is still arriving, instead of "Cache" -- the copy isn't on the device yet.

- Servers with more than one library -- a music library and a podcast library, say -- can be browsed a library at a time. The Server screen in Settings gets a Libraries card listing each library the server grants your account, with a tick per library; untick one and Home, Library and Search stop showing what is in it. Ticking all of them is the same as the server's own default, so a library added later shows up without being ticked. Changing the set clears the index and downloads it again for the libraries that remain, and searches answer from the server in the meantime rather than from the rows being thrown away. Servers with a single library don't get the card.
- The dot on the Settings server card now reports the connection rather than the login: green when the server answers and streaming is trusted, yellow when it answers but the connection is being treated as degraded, red when the last check got no answer at all. The "connected" word beside the account name follows it. Opening Settings or the Server screen checks at once, so the dot is about now and not the last minute's check. The card also carries a server icon.
- The app no longer opens with the connection wrongly treated as degraded. The first health check was being restarted while it was still in flight -- iOS announces the app entering the foreground on a cold launch too -- and the cut-short check was counted as a failed one; a second, genuinely slow check during startup then completed the pair that marks the connection degraded, and that verdict was saved for the next twelve hours of launches. A check that is cancelled, or that misses before iOS has reported what kind of connection the device is on, no longer counts against the connection.
- The Debug screen has a switch for a companion trigger panel. With it on, a small paw button sits over the app -- drag it anywhere, tap it open, and it plays one of Nutty's behaviours on demand instead of waiting out the clock behind it: the tap trick, the answer to a starred song, sitting down on the mini player's edge, the outing to an album's play button, the shove against a home screen shelf, and the look up out of a mini player the tab bar has folded away. Each button is only live where its behaviour can actually play -- the shelf push greys out while you are on an album, the outing greys out while you are not, everything greys out with the full player open -- so what is lit says where the companion is as much as what it can do. Off by default, and nothing about the companion changes when it is off.
- Settings has a Debug screen, below Accessibility, and "Download upcoming songs" has moved there out of Playback Settings' Debugging card. The switch itself is unchanged -- it is still on, and turning it off still makes every song stream as it starts -- but it was the one thing on the Playback screen nobody is meant to live with, and it sat at the bottom of the longest screen in Settings. It now has a place of its own, which is also where anything else of that kind will go. Playback Settings ends with the crossfade card again.
- The endless queue keeps going under the render engine. A song you put on runs past the end of what you started it with because the app quietly asks the server for similar songs and adds them to the end -- and on the render engine it never asked, so playback stopped at the last song of the album, playlist or search you had picked. The gathering stands down while a song is still arriving, so the round trips for suggestions aren't taken out of the audio, and on the system player it started again as soon as the next song loaded. A song streaming on the render engine is never reloaded that way, so nothing ever started it again. The wait now hands back to the gathering when it ends, wherever it ends -- which also fixes the same silent stop on a slow connection, where the system player waits for a download before it starts a song.
- The first song after opening the app is delivered the way Settings asks for it. iOS reports what kind of connection the device is on a moment after the app starts, and until it does SwiftDrome assumed a data plan -- the cautious guess, because guessing Wi-Fi would spend someone's data. Whichever song was loaded inside that moment, usually the one the app puts back on the player where you left it, was asked for as a transcode and stayed one for its whole runtime, so a library set to send the original file over Wi-Fi could still open with an Opus stream while every song after it arrived untouched. The connection is now read at startup rather than only waited for, and a song opened before the answer arrived is opened again the instant it does -- silently, since it is either still paused or a fraction of a second in.

## [0.22.0] - 2026-08-22

- Settings puts Storage right after Playback -- the two screens that matter while setting up streaming and downloads now sit together -- with Pets and Accessibility behind it. The playback-engine picker's summary is one line for both engines instead of changing with the choice, and the menu takes the width its labels need instead of hyphenating "Render (alpha)" mid-word.
- Playback Settings offers a second engine to play downloaded music with. "System player" is what SwiftDrome has always used and stays the default; "Render engine" is new, and it decodes downloaded songs in the app and hands them to an audio graph of its own, so the last sample of one song is followed by the first of the next with nothing at all in between. The old join was the closest thing the system's player has an API for -- close enough that most records sound continuous, and not close enough that a record mastered to run without a break always does. This one is not close: it is the same thing as no boundary. Songs already on the device play this way, and so do most songs still arriving over the network (the entry below says which); the two engines never sound at once.
- The render engine now plays songs that are still arriving over the network, off the same download the cache is keeping -- playback starts once a few seconds are on the device, and the boundary into the next downloaded song is the engine's own seamless join. This was the one place the engine still had the system player's audible gap: the moment a streamed song ended and a downloaded one began. It asks two things of the stream -- the server must send the file unchanged rather than transcoding it, and the format must be readable from its front, which FLAC, WAV and AIFF are; Opus and transcoded streams stay on the system player for now. Seeking inside a song that is still arriving hands that song to the system player, whose re-streamed seek lands exactly; a connection slower than the music pauses playback to buffer, the way a stream always has; and a stream the engine cannot start within a few seconds goes to the system player rather than being waited on in silence.
- The render engine streams transcoded music too: Opus, MP3 and FLAC transcodes play on it the same way untranscoded streams do, read as they arrive. A song delivered this way keeps the server's idea of its length rather than adopting the arriving file's guess, which for Ogg files has been wrong before. Two deliveries stay on the system player: AAC transcodes, whose file holds its table of contents until the end, and Ogg Vorbis, which the system cannot play at all.
- Seeking inside a song the render engine is streaming stays on the engine now. If the download has already finished in the background, the seek is served from the file on the device; a FLAC stream is rebuilt to start where the seek was aimed, landing inside a single frame -- the same machinery the system player has used for this; and a transcoded stream is asked for again starting at the target, with the fraction of a second the server cannot express decoded and dropped so the landing is exact. Before this, any seek in a streamed song quietly moved that song back to the system player for the rest of its runtime.
- Playback Settings ends with a small Debugging card holding one switch: "Download upcoming songs". It is on, and staying on is right for listening -- the lookahead is why album boundaries usually land between files already on the device. Turning it off makes every song stream as it starts, which is what testing streamed playback needs; downloads asked for by name still run.
- The new engine is worth calling experimental, and the picker says so. Two things it does not do yet: crossfading into a song that is not downloaded falls back to a plain gap between them, and two songs recorded at different sample rates are not joined to each other -- a boundary a record almost never has, since an album is one format throughout. Volume levelling, crossfading between downloaded songs, seeking, the lock screen and everything around them work as before. Anything the engine cannot take -- a surround recording, a file it cannot read -- is quietly handed back to the system player rather than failing.
- Seeking inside a downloaded song is exact under the new engine. It reads the file again from the frame the position names, where the system player works a position out from an assumed bitrate in some formats and lands somewhere else entirely -- the error SwiftDrome repacks downloaded FLACs to avoid. So is the length shown: the engine counted the song's frames to play them, rather than believing what the file claims about itself.
- The squirrel's flat-push animation got redrawn frames.

## [0.21.0] - 2026-08-22

- The companion pays the play button a visit. With an album screen open, every so often it dives head-first into the mini player, turns up hauling itself over the top of the album's round play button, taps it three times with a paw -- the button dips under each tap, and nothing starts playing; the queue is yours, not the squirrel's -- then climbs back down behind the button and is thrown back out of the mini player it went into. It goes from wherever it happens to be: pacing the pill, sitting on its rim while the music is paused (it stands up first), or waiting inside the tab bar's collapsed capsule, in which case it slips off unseen and comes home to waiting there. If the button scrolls away or the full player opens mid-visit, the climb is abandoned rather than played to a screen that has moved on.
- Multi-disc albums play in the order they were released. Track numbers start again at 1 on every disc, and the on-device library was sorting an album by track number alone -- so a two-disc release came back interleaved, disc 1 track 1, disc 2 track 1, disc 1 track 2, and played that way. On a record meant to run continuously that is audible immediately: a remix from the second disc wedged between two songs written to follow each other. Worse, the two tracks either side of that wedge looked consecutive by number, so the player joined them gaplessly -- a hard butt-join between two pieces of music that have nothing to do with each other. Discs are now carried through from the server and the album is ordered by disc first.
- An album on more than one disc shows them as separate sections, headed "Disc 1", "Disc 2" and so on. Albums on a single disc are unchanged -- a lone "Disc 1" over every record would be a heading that says nothing -- and so are albums whose server doesn't report disc numbers.
- The seam where one disc ends and the next begins gets a plain gap: no crossfade, and no gapless join. That is the one place a release really is two records -- the side ran out, and whoever sequenced it knew you would get up and change it. Blending across it invents a continuity the record never had, and joining it removes a gap that is part of the work. Landing part-way into another disc, which is a shuffle's doing rather than the record's, still blends like any other pair.
- Updating re-fetches the library once. The disc numbers aren't in the copy already downloaded to the device and can't be worked out from what is -- they only ever existed in a response the sync had thrown away -- so the app asks the server for them again, as a full sync on the next launch. While it runs, the browse screens read from the server exactly as they do before a library has ever been downloaded, so albums are in the right order straight away rather than after the sync finishes.
- A library that was never fully downloaded now finishes the job on the next launch instead of never. The app only ever chose the full download for a completely empty library; anything else got the quick check, which looks at the newest albums and stops. A first download that was interrupted -- closed the app, lost the connection -- was therefore never resumed, and whatever it had not reached stayed missing however many times the app was opened. The quick check also asks the right question now: it used to look up which albums were still owed track lists by taking the first few hundred alphabetically and seeing whether any of the new albums were among them, which for most libraries they were not, so it concluded there was nothing to fetch.
- Editing the queue while one song is fading into the next now takes effect. A crossfade starts several seconds before a track ends and stays running -- and audible -- for the whole of that overlap, and anything done to the queue inside that window was ignored: taking out the song that was fading in, dragging another ahead of it, queueing something to play next, or pressing shuffle or repeat all left the blend running to its end. You heard several seconds of a song you had just removed, and only once the overlap finished did the player notice and jump to the right one. The queue is now read again the moment it changes, a blend aimed at a song that is no longer next is called off, and whatever really does come next is faded in instead -- the same check a gapless album join already got.
- The log tells the truth about shuffle, and about a crossfade that gets called off. The line reporting a new queue carried a "shuffling: true" that read as "shuffle is on" and was there for every ordinary play whether it was on or off; it now says "shuffle off", "shuffle on, shuffled as it landed", or -- for a queue picked up from another device, which keeps the order it arrived in -- "shuffle on, arriving order kept". A crossfade dropped part-way through by a skip, a seek or the queue moving under it used to leave no trace at all, so the only blends in the log were the ones that ran all the way to the end; one that is called off now says so, and how far it had got.
- The queue is choosier about what it gathers when it runs on past the end of what you started, and Playback has a "Skip short tracks" slider that says how choosy. Anything shorter than the length it is set to is taken for an interlude and left out; the default has moved from a minute to a minute and a half, and sliding it to zero turns the length test off altogether. Titles are read as well as lengths, and more carefully than before: a track called "Intro", "Outro", "Interlude" or "Skit" is left out, as is one that ends with that as an aside -- "Grace (Interlude)", "Grace [Skit 2]", "Grace -- Intro" -- while a song that merely contains the word is still a song, so "Intro to Reality", "The Outro Song" and "Skittles" stay. Only what the queue picks for itself is judged this way: an album or playlist you put on plays through whole, interludes included.
- The library can be browsed while it is being downloaded for the first time. Albums, artists, songs, genres, playlists and favourites are read from the on-device index once it has been filled once -- but a first full sync takes a while, and until it finished those screens had nothing to show and said so, on a connection that was working perfectly well. They now fetch from the server in the meantime, exactly as they did before there was an index at all, and switch over to the index the moment the sync completes. An album opened during that first sync arrives with its track list rather than empty, since track lists are the last thing a sync gathers.
- The Downloaded screen shows what is on the device while that first sync is still running. Downloaded files are stored by id alone, so each one is looked up for its title and artwork -- against the index when it has the song, against the server when it doesn't, which before the first sync finishes is every one of them. Those lookups all went out at once, queued behind the sync's own requests and timed out, and a lookup that failed took its row off the screen with it: a full set of downloads read as "nothing downloaded". They now go out a few at a time and each row appears as it resolves, and one that can't be looked up no longer removes the others.
- A small pixel-art companion now lives on top of the mini player: it wanders back and forth while music plays, and tapping it plays a quick crouch-jump-roll-land trick before it goes back to wandering. It hides along with the mini player itself while nothing is playing, while the full player is open, and while the mini player is minimized into the tab bar. Settings has a Pets screen for it: a switch to turn the companion off entirely, and the choice of which one keeps you company -- one to pick from today, shown animating in the list rather than just named.
- The companion on the mini player has more to do than pace. Stop the music and it walks to a halt, sits down on the rim of the pill and lets its legs hang over the edge; it gets up a beat after playback comes back, or straight away if you tap it, since a tap asked for the trick rather than for a get-up. Star what is playing and it turns and beats a heart -- every time the heart is pressed, on a song or on a whole album -- and while something you had already starred is playing it notices on its own now and again, at most once every forty seconds, so it stays company rather than a notification.
- Closing the full player hands the mini player a title that reads from its first word. The scrolling title and artist in the pill kept running the whole time the player was open, so the moment the player landed the line jumped to wherever the scroll had got to in the meantime -- often the middle of a word. Both lines now start their run over as the pill comes back.
- The companion leaves the mini player under its own steam when the tab bar folds the pill into its inline capsule. Instead of blinking out of existence, it turns to face the middle of the screen, gathers itself and dives for it, falls the gap down to wherever the row is on its way to, and disappears head-first through the edge of it. Whatever it was doing gives way to that -- a walk, a sit on the rim, a beat of the idle -- because the row it is standing on is already moving. Scroll back up before it has gone under and it breaks off the dive and carries on pacing; scroll back up after and it is standing where it went in.
- The companion has something to do while the mini player is folded away in the tab bar. Every so often it puts its head up over the edge of the collapsed pill for a look around -- somewhere new along it each time, since nothing could see where it went -- and ducks back in. Scroll the mini player back out and it waits for the pill to arrive back where it belongs before coming out of it, thrown up off the row and landing on its feet on top of it, as though the pill rising had flung it out.

## [0.20.0] - 2026-08-18

- Music playing somewhere else can be picked up here. When another device is on a track -- the Navidrome web player in another room, a second phone, anything that reports to the server -- Home shows a banner naming it and what it is on, and Claim carries on from the second it had reached. Sessions that are paused are offered too, not only playing ones, since a paused session is exactly the one you walked away from. The other device keeps going: there is no way to pause another client over the Subsonic API, so this takes the session over rather than moving it, and the banner doesn't pretend otherwise. What comes across depends on what the other end published. Between two SwiftDrome devices the queue arrives with the track, once the device you are leaving has played something since it launched -- before that it has nothing published to hand over. From a client that never saves a play queue, and neither the Navidrome web player nor Supersonic ever does, there is no queue on the server to read: the track and its position transfer, and the banner says so before the tap rather than leaving it to be discovered afterwards. This device publishes its own session the same way, so the handoff works in both directions, and it runs whatever the scrobble setting says -- that setting governs play history alone. The banner can be dismissed, and pulling down to refresh Home brings it back if that session is still running; the app stops looking for sessions while it is in the background.
- With Left-Handed Controls on, the line under the artwork on the Now Playing screen mirrors: the year and genre move to the right, the heart and the ellipsis menu to the left, under the thumb. It is the same flip the album screen's action row already made, and only the two blocks swap sides -- the text inside each keeps its own reading order.
- AAC is still listed as a transcoding format but can no longer be picked. A server transcoding on the fly hands AAC over as ADTS, which carries nothing to say where the encoder's padding ends, so every track starts with about a twentieth of a second of silence that no amount of scheduling removes -- gapless playback is impossible in that format, not merely worse. The row stays in the menu, greyed, so the answer to "where did AAC go?" is on screen rather than absent, and anyone whose setting already reads AAC keeps it: what changed is that there is no way back to it.
- Each device registers with Navidrome under its own name. Since iOS 16 a device reports itself generically as "iPhone" unless an app asks Apple for the entitlement to read the real name, so two of the same phone arrived at the server as one player, each overwriting what the other was doing. The app now appends four characters unique to the install -- "SwiftDrome (iPhone·a1b2)" -- which is what shows up in Navidrome's Players list.

A song that is still streaming is fetched from the byte your seek points at, which lands within a tenth of a second. Nothing on the server is touched, re-encoded or renamed — this happens on the device, to the app's own copy.
- Reopening the app returns to where you left a lossless song, rather than to wherever the seek happened to land. The check meant to catch a restored position that missed was trusting a clock that reports the position it was asked for, so it never found anything to correct.
- A download that arrives incomplete no longer becomes a permanently broken song. Two ways a bad file used to end up in the cache looking like a good one: a server answering with an error page instead of the track, stored as if it were music, and a connection closing part-way through a transfer the app had no length to check it against. Either one is poison once cached — a half-finished FLAC still claims the full length in its header, so it plays out early, skips, or lands somewhere else every time you scrub. Downloads are now checked against the file size and length the server reports for the track, and anything short is discarded instead of kept.
- The app has a real icon. Installed builds used to sit on the home screen as a blank placeholder, because nothing in the build carried an icon at all; SwiftDrome now ships its own mark — two triangles for the outgoing and incoming track, with the overlap where both are audible drawn in its own lighter colour. The same mark greets you on the login screen in place of the stock music symbol, and the AltStore source now shows it as the app's icon and header rather than two broken images.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-08-05

### One track into the next

- Tracks now crossfade: the next one starts before the current one ends, with the outgoing track fading out as the incoming one fades in. The overlap is 6 seconds by default and can be set anywhere from 0 to 12, or turned off, on the new Playback screen in Settings.
- Consecutive album tracks are joined gaplessly instead of blended, so records mastered as one continuous piece finally play as one. Only a genuine continuation is joined — track 1 into track 3 was never meant to butt together, so it gets the crossfade like any other transition, as does a track the server sends no number for.
- A gapless join needs the server's original file or an Opus stream: any other transcode on the way out strips the information a seamless join depends on. Wi-Fi asks for the original file by default, so that's where it applies unless you change it.
- Playback no longer runs out. The queue keeps itself stocked with ten tracks similar to what you're listening to, topped up as they play, so a single song can carry on indefinitely. Where a server has no similar tracks to offer, it falls back to other songs by the artists you're playing, drawn from a random album of theirs rather than the same few tracks every time. Starting from an album still queues the rest of that album first.

### Now Playing

- The screen has been rebuilt around the crossfade, which you can now see happening: the cover art of the track coming in dissolves across the one going out, the background colour turns over with it, and a moving indicator in the metadata line tracks the blend. The tail of the progress bar is marked, so you can see where the next track will start blending in.
- It says what you're actually hearing — genre and year above the progress bar, and beneath it whether the track is streaming or playing from the device, its format, bitrate and ReplayGain. The codec named is the one reaching you rather than the format of the file on the server, which differ whenever the server is transcoding, and the bit rate is shown only where it describes the stream.
- The background no longer drops to black for a moment when a track changes. Cover art is kept in memory, so it also appears instantly on screens you've already visited.
- The buffering notice sits above the progress bar instead of below the times, where it pushed the controls down as it came and went.
- Added a sleep timer: 15, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, after which playback pauses where it got to, so pressing play carries on.
- The layout follows Apple Music more closely. The volume slider is gone, play/pause has lost its surrounding circle, and the row along the bottom is AirPlay, download state, lyrics, queue and the sleep timer, with favourite and a menu button above the progress bar.
- Dragging the progress bar no longer fights you: the handle stays where you put it while you drag, and playback jumps once you let go. The lock screen and Control Center bar follows playback and can be dragged too, and the two stay in step after a stall or a buffer.
- Reopening the app brings back what you were listening to: the miniplayer shows the track paused where you left off, and pressing play carries on from there.

### Queue

- The queue is one list in the order you'll hear it, rather than three sections named after where the app happened to be holding each track. What's playing is marked with a level meter over its artwork and the screen opens scrolled to it, with the last five you played above.
- Tapping anything in the queue plays it straight away. The tracks between it and what's playing are dropped rather than skipped through, and the queue tops itself back up afterwards.
- Reordering needs no Edit button. Tracks you queued yourself carry a grip on the right: press it and the row lifts with a tap you can feel, follows your finger straight up and down without wandering sideways, and ticks as it passes each track it displaces. Pressing anywhere else on a row opens a menu to play it now, move it up next, favourite it, or remove it, and the list still scrolls normally. Anything you add with Play Next stays ahead of the automatically gathered tracks.
- A labelled rule marks where your queue ends and the tracks that follow on their own begin — those can be removed but not reordered.
- Swiping a track away removes it, on the tracks still to come rather than on ones already played, and the row goes with it. A track you remove from the continuous queue is not offered again the next time the queue tops itself up.
- Your queue survives closing the app. Reopening restores what was playing along with everything queued behind it, what had already been played, and the tracks gathered to follow on.

### Streaming and storage

- Streaming quality is now set per connection. Wi-Fi and cellular each choose what to ask the server for — the original file, or Opus, AAC or MP3 — and a bitrate from 96 up to 320 kbps when transcoding. A line on the same screen says what all of it adds up to on the connection you're on right now.
- Seeking is immediate on Wi-Fi: the track is quietly downloaded while it plays, so dragging the progress bar has the whole song to work with. On cellular, and anywhere Low Data Mode is on, nothing is downloaded speculatively — seeking there fetches the track at the moment you ask for it, showing "Buffering to seek…" for the moment that takes, after which every later seek in that track is instant.
- The most recently played tracks are kept on the device, so replaying a song or skipping back to it plays from local storage instead of downloading it again.
- Storage has a maximum size — 512 MB up to 10 GB — with a meter showing how much of it is in use. Those recently played tracks aren't governed by it and are always kept; Clear Cache removes them along with everything else.

- Settings opens with the server you're connected to: which one, the account it's using, whether it's answering, and how many custom headers are set.
